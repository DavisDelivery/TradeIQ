=== TRADEIQ — WHICH BOARD CAN YOU TRUST? (backtest audit, 2026-08-06) ===

## The answer

**None of them. Not one board in this app has a validated edge, and the ones that have been measured properly all lost to buying SPY and sitting on it.**

The board you can trust *most* is **trend** — but only because it is the one board honest enough to tell you it doesn't work. It measures no edge, ships no score, and its registry row deliberately stores `null` so the UI cannot print a flattering number. That is trustworthy *reporting*, not a trustworthy *signal*.

If the question is "which board is likely to put me on a winner": **the evidence does not support any of them**, and two of the boards that look best in the app right now (Prophet's +80.9pp, Insiders' #1 league ranking) are the two whose numbers collapse hardest under inspection.

Three corrections to the framing before the table:

1. **The negatives are not proven either.** Decomposing the three flagship losses into monthly active return against SPY, using the only tracking-error estimate the repo ever produced (sd 3.637%/mo, n=83, `reports/fable/design.md:203`): williams t = −1.30, target t = −1.32, fable t = −1.30. **None rejects zero alpha.** The correct reading is "no evidence of edge," not "edge disproved." The only run in the entire repo that clears |t| ≥ 2 is `bt_20260519115311_sguabi` (−117.66pp, t = −2.49) — and it is undocumented and in no registry row. So: the boards aren't proven losers, they're *unproven*, and the losses are large enough (−73 to −101 pp cumulative) that betting on them is unjustified regardless.
2. **The "we handicapped ourselves with survivorship bias and still lost" defence is gone.** `netlify/functions/shared/universe-history.ts` holds 101 point-in-time sp500 snapshots (2018-01-31 → 2026-05-14) — SIVB, FRC, SBNY, TWTR, ATVI all present and correctly removed on their real dates. The main-engine runs are genuinely PIT-corrected. `docs/BACKTEST_LIMITATIONS.md` §2 is stale and says otherwise. The results have to stand on their own.
3. **The four registry numbers are not comparable to each other.** Different cadence (weekly/monthly/quarterly), different selection semantics (BUY-only vs score-ranked), different cost models, and — critically — **different engines**: williams (2026-05-19) and lynch (2026-06-08) predate the delisting-realization fix (b93eb3e / 4ce70b5, 2026-06-10/11), under which a delisted holding silently returned 0% instead of going to zero. Their losses are, if anything, understated.

## What a 3-year backtest would and would not add

**It is the wrong instrument. Do not spend the compute on it expecting an answer.**

What it would add: nothing that changes a ranking.

- 2023-08-01 → 2026-08-01 = 1,096 days = **37 monthly rebalances** (vs 84 for the existing 7-year runs). Independent observations are rebalances, not trades — 20 simultaneous positions in one market factor are roughly one bet, not 20. You'd have 44% of the sample and 1.51× wider standard errors.
- At the tracking error these runs actually exhibit (~12%/yr), the SE of annualized excess over 3 years is **6.9pp/yr**. A single board needs **+13.6pp/yr of excess CAGR** to clear 95% significance. The best number ever produced in this repo — Prophet's +80.9pp over 8 years — is ~+7.3pp/yr, and it's an artifact.
- To rank boards *against each other*: the measured spread among them is ~3.4pp/yr (williams 4.38% CAGR, target 4.29%, lynch ~0.96%). At ρ=0.5 correlation between two long-only top-20 S&P portfolios, separating a 3.4pp/yr gap at 95% takes **~48 years of data**. Ranking six boards with a Bonferroni correction needs 18.2pp/yr over 3 years.
- Every window ending 2026-08 currently stamps `universeSurvivorshipCorrected: false`, because PIT snapshots stop 2026-05-14 and `universe-pool.ts:112-130` is strict all-or-nothing. The last ~3 rebalances would silently trade May membership.

What it *would* legitimately add — and this is the only honest reason to run one:

- A **regime check**. The 7-year runs are dominated by the 2023-24 mega-cap melt-up, which is exactly where these breadth-seeking boards bleed. A 3-year run tells you whether the loss is uniform or concentrated. That is descriptive, not inferential — no t-stat attaches to it.

And it can't cover the whole app anyway. `backtest-runs-trigger.ts:158-164` hard-codes `SUPPORTED_BOARDS = [prophet, williams, lynch, target, earnings, fable]`. **catalyst and insider return HTTP 400** (no PIT scorer — `score-at-date.ts:1141-1148` falls through to `return null`). **trend and vector have no engine path at all.** And prophet can't take an arbitrary window — its harness accepts fixed labels only, and its ranker is broken (see below). So "backtest all the boards" is at most 4 comparable runs, not 12.

## Boards presented in a way the evidence does not support

Four, in order of severity.

**1. Prophet — the only positive verdict in the app, and it is not a measurement of Prophet.**
The chip shows +80.9pp vs SPY. The run (`pb-full-202605182201-uohfwr`) bought 14 names on 2018-01-01 (MU, WMB, AMD, EOG, AAPL, TXN, VLO, PANW, UNH, QCOM, ADM, MNST, GWW, MO) and **never traded again** — 418 rebalances, 1 trade date, 0 sells, identical `candidatesConsidered: 14` on every single date because one stale Firestore snapshot resolved for all eight years. 6 of 20 slots sat in cash the whole time and are in the equity denominator. This is exactly the "concentration luck, not active signal" defect that Phase 4i claimed to fix by moving v1→v2; it survived the version bump. The "4/8 rolling windows" qualifier is the **modal outcome of a fair coin** (two-sided p = 1.000, t on the eight excesses = 0.14) — and one of the four "wins" is `pb-rolling-2018-202608052200-srxbbj`, a **100%-cash year** (portfolioReturnPct 0.000) credited as a win by `portfolio-verdict.ts:133-136` purely because SPY fell 7%. Strip it: 3/7. `runId` in the registry is `null`, and `VerdictChip.jsx:32` silently omits the Run line rather than flagging the missing attribution. Forward test today: **−2.87pp alpha, alphaWinRate 26%.**

**2. Insiders — ranked at/near #1 in the live league on an effective sample of two.**
20 of its 22 picks entered on the same day. That is one draw of a 20-name basket, not 22 draws. SE = 3.54pp, t = 0.86 on the +3.05pp. And the league it tops is pure noise end to end: 25 entries, mean rankScore −0.025pp, sd 1.776; under the null that every board has identical true alpha, expected max = +3.19 (observed +3.95) and expected min = −3.24 (observed −3.18, matching to 0.06pp). Insider's +3.05 is actually *below* the noise-expected maximum. The league also sorts one scalar across two incompatible statistics (11 entries "avg alpha @ 7d", 14 "unrealized alpha, open cohort") and ranks an n=1 cohort at #9. The board sits in the **default nav** (`src/App.jsx:97`) with **no VerdictChip**, and it ranks on raw `buyDollars` — a size-biased sort, not a score, with the C-suite role branch effectively dead (`insider-provider.ts:286-295`).

**3. Catalyst, Insiders, Earnings, Sentiment sit in the default nav with no verdict chip at all.**
None is in the `VerdictBoard` union, so `VerdictChip.jsx:28` returns null and the user sees a clean board with no warning. Catalyst's composite launders two dead channels (patent and contracts both at confidence 0.1, contributing nothing while still normalizing the score scale — verified live: ELV composite 76). Earnings has a **forward-test contamination bug**: `forward-test.ts:64` is `{board:'earnings', universe:'all', take:20}` with **no long/short filter**, while the file's own comment says the filter exists to stop non-buy rows poisoning a long cohort. Live board today: 26 directional_long, **9 directional_short, 1 short_volatility** — and short_volatility scores 75, so it ranks *high* into the top 20. The +0.16pp is a mixed long/short basket reported as long-only. Top row today is SLSN at $1.06, untradeable at the modelled costs.

**4. The registry's prose over-claims in the one direction it thinks it's being conservative.**
`verdicts.ts` for target says the scores "rank stocks worse than random" (IC −0.0105). No standard error for IC is computed anywhere in this codebase — realistic SE is 0.011–0.022, so t ≈ −0.5 to −1.0, CI comfortably spanning zero. It is also directly contradicted by seven completed russell2k target runs at **IC +0.0461** — runs the registry still claims "did not complete" (`verdicts.ts:142-144`). Lynch's IC is printed to four digits on a quantity with SE ≈ 0.04. The net effect: **the registry is admirably conservative about claiming edge and reckless about claiming its absence.** "NO_EDGE" rows read to a user as *measurements of zero*; every one of them is a *failure to measure*.

## Bottom line

Your app's boards, where they have been measured at all, returned **4.3–4.4% CAGR against SPY's ~11%** over seven years — roughly a third of buy-and-hold, net of costs, on a properly survivorship-corrected universe. Those shortfalls are not statistically significant (t ≈ −1.3), which means the honest statement is not "these boards are proven to lose" but "**seven years of data cannot distinguish them from a strategy that loses 6% a year to the index, and nothing here distinguishes them from each other.**"

The one board with a positive number is measuring a basket someone effectively bought once in 2018 and forgot about. The one board topping the live league has two independent observations. The one board you can genuinely trust is trend — and what it trustworthily reports is that its signal doesn't work.

**Trust none of them to put you on a winner. The instrument that could earn that trust — the forward test with matured, costed, date-deduplicated cohorts — exists, is running, and needs about a year.**
